     independent trapezoidal tau.
  G  convergence: n_path 192->384, n_s 64->128, E nodes 120->239, compared by
     log-log interpolation to the SAME energies.  Worst change 1.7 % (at the
     10 keV transparency minimum, where interpolation curvature dominates);
     < 0.5 % outside the 0.5-10 keV band; < 2e-05 above 1 GeV.
     QUOTED NUMERICAL UNCERTAINTY: 0.5 %, rising to 2 % in 0.5-10 keV.

Window sensitivity (z>5.5 -> z>3.0), photons at z_i=20
  1 keV +0.0 %   89 keV +1.9 %   1 MeV +6.5 %   100 MeV +4.9 %
  BUT 10.5 keV +58 % -- inside the transparency window the answer depends on
  how long the photon is allowed to keep travelling.  Quote with care there.

Inherited caveat from the ELECTRON table (stage2_cascade.py)
  Electron-radiated photons are transported only to E_gamma = 3e4 eV, where
  the PHOTOIONIZATION probability is < 1e-3.  Compton absorption of those
  photons is not in the electron table.  Above K ~ 7e8 eV the inverse-Compton
  photons exceed 3e4 eV, which is why the electron curve saturates at
  N = 4.1e6 (z=20) / 4.5e6 (z=10).  The electron curve is therefore itself a
  mild lower bound above ~7e8 eV.


================================================================================
STAGE 9 -- WHY UV PHOTONS REIONIZE THE UNIVERSE AND ENERGETIC PARTICLES DO NOT
================================================================================
Scripts : stage9_budget.py -> stage9_budget.log
          stage9_figure.py -> manuscript/figures_redo/
                              fig_reionization_budget.{pdf,png}
Write-up: Sect. 5 of ionizations_per_primary_response.tex / .pdf

The stage8 figure is PER PRIMARY PARTICLE.  Reionization is decided by
    N_ion = (1/W)  x  (energy available in that channel),
and BOTH factors favour the ultraviolet.  There is no inconsistency with the
standard picture; the figure quantifies it.

1) IONIZATIONS PER ERG (= 1/W, read off stage8 at z_i = 10)
     channel                    W [eV]      per erg      rel. to 20 eV UV
     UV photon, 20 eV           20.4        3.06e+10     1.000
     UV photon, 30 eV           28.5        2.19e+10     0.717
     soft X-ray, 0.5 keV        35.7        1.75e+10     0.572
     X-ray, 1 keV               52.5        1.19e+10     0.389
     X-ray, 3 keV               1076        5.80e+08     0.019
     hard X-ray, 10 keV         3.21e+04    1.95e+07     6.4e-04
     electron, 1 keV            35.4        1.77e+10     0.577
     electron, 1 MeV            149         4.20e+09     0.137
     CR electron, 1 GeV         225         2.77e+09     0.091
     CR electron, 1 TeV         2.24e+05    2.78e+06     9.1e-05
     HMXB power law G=2, 0.5-8 keV:  W_eff = 110 eV,  0.186 x UV
   (i)   A threshold photon is the perfect ioniser: W = B_H = 13.6 eV is the
         thermodynamic optimum.  A fast electron cannot reach it because ~1/3
         of its energy goes to collisional excitation and ~1/3 to heat
         (Shull & van Steenberg 1985; Valdes & Ferrara 2008; Furlanetto &
         Stoever 2010) -> the 35 eV floor.  UV beats the best electron by ~2x.
   (ii)  Above 2 keV the IGM stops absorbing (our sigma_pi = sigma_C at 2333 eV;
         P_int 1.00 -> 0.157 between 2 and 10 keV).  Hard X-rays do not ionize
         inefficiently, they do not interact at all (Oh 2001; Venkatesan et al.
         2001; McQuinn 2012; Mesinger, Ferrara & Spiegel 2013).
   (iii) GeV electrons are ALSO poor per erg (W = 225 eV) because they cool by
         inverse Compton into photons above 3e4 eV, i.e. into the same
         transparency window.  Same failure mode.

2) ENERGY AVAILABLE PER MSUN OF STAR FORMATION
     stellar H-ionizing UV (intrinsic)         1.39e+50 erg/Msun
       ... escaping, f_esc = 0.1               1.39e+49
     HMXB X-rays, L_X/SFR = 3e39               9.5e+46
     HMXB X-rays, L_X/SFR = 1e40               3.2e+47
     cosmic rays, all species  [MEASURED]      1.44e+48
     cosmic-ray ELECTRONS      [MEASURED]      2.07e+46  (range 1.5-2.9e46)
   Inputs: xi_ion = 10^25.2 Hz/erg (Robertson+2015; Bouwens+2016);
   L_UV/SFR from Kennicutt 1998 / Madau & Dickinson 2014; <E_gamma> = 20 eV;
   f_esc = 0.1 (Robertson+2015; Finkelstein+2019); L_X/SFR from Mineo+2012,
   Fragos+2013, Lehmer+2021.  The two COSMIC-RAY rows are MEASURED, not
   assumed -- see the provenance block at the end of this section.
   NOTE the deliberate f_esc asymmetry: the same E^-3 cross section that makes
   the IGM transparent to X-rays makes the HOST transparent too, so the hard
   channels escape freely.  Worth ~10x to them -- still not enough.

3) IONIZATIONS DELIVERED PER MSUN  (= 1 x 2)
     escaping stellar UV                 4.26e+59     1
     HMXB X-rays (L_X/SFR = 1e40)        1.79e+57     4.2e-03
     HMXB X-rays (L_X/SFR = 3e39)        5.38e+56     1.3e-03

   FLAG: no proton cascade table exists here; the CR-proton row is indicative
   only.  It agrees with the published limit "no more than a few per cent"
   (Sazonov & Sunyaev 2015; Leite et al. 2017).  IMPORTANT: the CR literature
   is about PROTONS -- electrons carry only K_ep = 1.2-2.1 % of the injected CR
   power (MEASURED, Strong+2010 Table 2), so the stage8 electron curve has
   almost no counterpart in a real source budget.

   X-ray SED scan at L_X/SFR = 1e40 (E_min 0.2-1 keV, Gamma 1.5-2.5):
     0.2 keV / 2.5  ->  9.3e-03 x UV   (most favourable corner)
     1.0 keV / 1.5  ->  1.2e-03 x UV
   Reaching the ~10 % X-ray share sometimes quoted needs f_esc(UV) ~ 0.01 or
   L_X/SFR ~ 1e42 -- the latter is what the unresolved soft X-ray background
   excludes (McQuinn 2012).  Mesinger+2013: X-ray feedback can sustain a ~10 %
   ionized IGM but cannot complete reionization.

4) TWO ARGUMENTS THAT ARE NOT ABOUT EFFICIENCY
   Self-limitation: as x_e rises, less of a fast electron's energy goes into
   ionization and more into heat.  Our OWN stage5 lock table shows W(1 keV)
   going 35.3 -> 89.7 eV as x_e goes 1e-4 -> 0.1.  A UV photon suffers no such
   penalty.  Hard channels drive a partially ionized, pre-heated IGM (their
   accepted 21-cm role) but cannot take x_e to 1.
   Photon-starvation: 2 ionizing photons per H with f_esc = 0.1 needs
   rho_* = 2.6e7 Msun/Mpc^3 comoving vs ~1e7 observed at z~6 (Madau &
   Dickinson 2014) -- the budget only just closes (Bolton & Haehnelt 2007), so
   there is no room for a large extra channel.  Reionization also ENDS fast, by
   z = 5.3 (Bosman et al. 2022) with tau_e = 0.054 (Planck 2018): a sharp
   ionization-front transition, not the smooth partial ionization a hard-photon
   model gives.

5) CONSISTENCY VERDICT
   Fully consistent.  Read per erg (panel b) instead of per particle (panel a),
   the stage8 figure says the optimum ioniser is a photon at the H threshold.
   The apparent electron superiority above ~1 keV is TRANSPORT, not efficiency:
   a keV electron stops locally, a keV photon has a near-Hubble mfp.  BELOW
   500 eV the ordering reverses and the PHOTON wins, by exactly one extra
   ionization (+27 % at 90 eV).  The 3-30 keV transparency minimum, where a
   photon injected at z=10 makes less than one ionization, is the quantitative
   form of "the IGM is transparent to hard X-rays".

PROVENANCE OF THE COSMIC-RAY ELECTRON ENERGY BUDGET
  (script: stage9_verify_budget.py -> stage9_verify_budget.log)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The value first used in stage9, 1e46 erg/Msun, was NOT quoted by any paper: it
was the product K_ep * eps_CR * E_SN / M_per_SN = 0.01 * 0.1 * 1e51 / 100.
It has since been REPLACED by a measured value.  Both routes are kept.

ROUTE 1 -- MEASURED (now the default in stage9_budget.py)
  Strong, Porter, Digel et al. (2010) ApJL 722, L58, Table 2, "Luminosity of
  the Galaxy for various processes, 1e38 erg/s", INJECTED cosmic-ray power
  (0.1-100 GeV), six propagation models (DR 1-3, PD 1-3):
      COSMIC RAYS total     805  790  698  780  723  660   (x1e38 erg/s)
        Protons             737  724  633  718  662  601
        Helium               56   55   48   52.4 48.3 43.9
        Leptons              12.2 14.5 16.9  10.04 12.91 15.15
          Primary e-          8.8 11.1 13.4   8.65 10.5  12.7
  Their Fig. 2 caption quotes the headline total as 7.9e40 erg/s.
  => MEASURED K_ep = L(primary e-)/L(protons) = 0.0119 - 0.0212.
  Milky Way SFR = 1.65 +/- 0.19 Msun/yr (Licquia & Newman 2015, ApJ 806, 96).
  => E_CR /M_* = 1.26 - 1.54e48 erg/Msun     (best value 1.44e48)
     E_CRe/M_* = 1.65 - 2.56e46 erg/Msun     (best value 2.07e46;
                 1.5 - 2.9e46 including the +/-12 % SFR error)

ROUTE 2 -- ASSUMPTION CHAIN (kept as an independent cross-check)
  E_SN = 1e51 erg per CCSN (Woosley & Weaver 1995, ApJS 101, 181).
  M_*/N_CCSN RECOMPUTED HERE from the IMF definitions, 8-100 Msun progenitors,
  IMF integrated 0.1-100 Msun:
      Chabrier (2003, PASP 115, 763):  84.75 Msun per CCSN  (1.18 per 100 Msun)
      Salpeter (1955, ApJ 121, 161) : 134.7  Msun per CCSN  (0.74 per 100 Msun)
    -> the "1 CCSN per 100 Msun" shorthand is the Chabrier result to 15 %.
  eps_CR = 0.1 (range 0.1-0.5): Drury, Markiewicz & Voelk (1989) A&A 225, 179;
    Berezinskii et al. (1990); Caprioli & Spitkovsky (2014) ApJ 783, 91 -- as
    collected verbatim by Gessey-Jones et al. (2023) MNRAS 526, 4262: "cosmic
    rays carry away between 10 and 50 per cent of the initial kinetic energy of
    the shock ... The majority of this energy is in cosmic ray protons, with
    per cent level portions of the energy in the form of alpha particles and
    electrons."
  => E_CRe/M_* = 1.18e46 (Chabrier) or 7.4e45 (Salpeter) erg/Msun.

AGREEMENT: routes 1 and 2 agree to a factor 1.7.  The originally used 1e46 was
LOW by ~2x, i.e. conservative.  Using the measured value moves the CR electron
share of the ionizations from 6.5e-5 to 1.35e-04 (range 1.0-1.9e-04) of the
escaping-UV share.  CONCLUSION UNCHANGED.

CAVEAT [FLAGGED]: the Milky Way is a present-day, high-metallicity, strongly
magnetised disc.  At z~10 both the SN energy per Msun (top-heavier IMF) and the
CR escape fraction into the IGM (smaller haloes, weaker fields; Leite et al.
2017) are plausibly larger, and K_ep ~ 0.1 is sometimes used for extragalactic
sources (Merten et al. 2017, Astropart. Phys. 90, 75).  Each is worth about one
order of magnitude, not four: even a 10x enhancement leaves CR electrons at
~1e-3 of the UV ionizations.
