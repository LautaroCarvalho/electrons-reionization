Recent cosmological analyses combining high-redshift cosmic microwave background (CMB) measurements with
low-redshift baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) data have reported a preference for dynamical dark energy, with
the cosmological constant scenario (ΛCDM) disfavored at the ∼ 3σ level. These analyses, however, typically rely
on large-scale CMB polarization measurements to constrain the optical depth to reionization τreio ∼ 0.06, raising
the question of whether potential systematics in this dataset could influence the inferred cosmological preference.
Excluding large-scale polarization data substantially weakens the tension with ΛCDM to the ≲ 2σ level, nevertheless
at a price of increasing τreio significantly to ∼ 0.09. Here, we use a physically motivated Gompertzian reionization
framework to perform a self-consistent Bayesian analysis combining CMB (excluding large-scale polarization data),
BAO, and independent measurements of the neutral hydrogen fraction evolution from quasar damping wing observations and dark pixel constraints. We derive τreio = 0.067 ± 0.011 (dynamical dark energy scenario) in good agreement
with cosmological analyses that would include large-scale CMB polarization data, while the inferred reionization
history is consistent with multiple observational constraints. Our analysis recovers a preference for dynamical dark
energy at the ⪆ 2σ level. These results demonstrate that astrophysical probes of reionization independently recover
the optical depth required by CMB polarization measurements, suggesting that potential systematics in large-scale
polarization alone are unlikely to fully explain the emerging preference for dynamical dark energy.

Appendix A Gompertzian reionization

1 Introduction
The standard cosmological model (ΛCDM) with its cosmological constant has been remarkably successful in describing the evolution of the Universe and a wide range
of cosmological observables, such as the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB). However, recent measurements from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument
(DESI; [1]) indicate a preference for dynamical dark energy (w0 wa CDM), leading to an emerging ∼ 3σ tension
with ΛCDM [2]1) .
In this work, we investigate the exclusion of largescale CMB polarization data as a possible mechanism
for resolving this tension [5-9]. Excluding these data allows the optical depth to reionization τreio to increase to
∼ 0.09. Since the CMB-inferred τreio is negatively correlated with the total matter density, Ωm , a higher value
of τreio can accommodate a lower Ωm , thus reducing the
discrepancy between early and late-time measurements
of Ωm [10] and potentially alleviating the H0 tension simultaneously [11]. However, large-scale CMB polarization data, last measured by the Planck collaboration [12]
and routinely included in modern CMB analyses, tightly
constrain τreio to ∼ 0.06 [13, 14]2) , hence preventing the
exploitation of this degeneracy. Furthermore, when the
interplay between reionization and cosmological parameters is modeled self-consistently, the τreio −Ωm degeneracy
becomes significantly weaker (see Figure 1 of [18]).
Motivated by the large value of τreio reported by [5,6],
several mechanisms have been proposed or revisited to
explain such an increase. For instance, Ref. [19] introduces inflationary features that enhance the value of τreio ,

000000-9
Ref. [20] considers the impact of infrared cutoff models,
and Ref. [21] suggests that supermassive Population III
stars could produce an early contribution to reionization
while remaining consistent with constraints from the end
stages of reionization.
Refs. [5, 6] showed that excluding large-scale CMB
polarization data relaxes the 3σ tension with ΛCDM
to ≲ 2σ. At the same time, it alleviates the apparent preference for negative neutrino masses and weak∑
ens the exclusion of the mν ≥ 0.06 eV lower bound implied by neutrino oscillation experiments, which is otherwise disfavored by approximately ∼ 3σ [18,22,23]. Measuring CMB polarization is considerably challenging as
the signal is several orders of magnitude weaker than
temperature anisotropies, requiring careful treatment of
instrumental systematics and astrophysical foregrounds
[13, 24-26]. Nevertheless, no evidence of unresolved systematic effects in the low-ℓ EE power spectrum has been
reported to date.
Among the primary free parameters of the standard
cosmological model, τreio remains the only one that has
not yet been measured with percent-level precision (see
Figure 1 in [27] for recent developments). It quantifies the cumulative Thomson scattering experienced by
CMB photons as they propagate towards our telescopes.
As such, τreio is not a fundamental cosmological parameter but rather an effective quantity determined by the
ionization history of the Universe. Consequently, it can
technically be demoted to derived parameter rather than
a free parameter [18, 27, 28]. Its inferred value depends
on the assumed reionization history xH i (z).

As an alternative to simulation-informed reionization
models, such as the Gompertzian prescription or neutral
network-based approaches [28], Refs. [8,9] reconstructed
the reionization history directly from publicly available
xH i measurements. This strategy implicitly treats all xH i
observations as equally reliable, although their robustness can vary significantly (e.g., [36]). Moreover, these
generic reconstructions do not incorporate physical insights from our current understanding of the reionization
process. Nevertheless, both studies found that including
a broad compilation of xH i constraints favors low values
of the optical depth, thus preserving the ΛCDM tension.
Even if some issue were identified in the large-scale
CMB polarization data, current EoR observations appear broadly consistent with the optical depths inferred
from those measurements [9]. Consequently, any systematic affecting the low-ℓ CMB EE power spectrum
would also need to be accompanied by inconsistencies in
multiple independent EoR observations. In this work, we
investigate whether including robust EoR constraints –
while still excluding large-scale CMB polarization data
– and adopting the physically motivated Gompertzian
reionization model alters the reported relaxation of the
tension with ΛCDM.

2 Data and Method
For BAO, we use the DESI DR2 measurements [2]. For
the high-ℓ CMB TT, TE, and EE likelihood, we adopt
the combination of Planck PR4 [37] and ACT DR6 [14],
following [14]. We additionally include the Planck PR3
large-scale (2 ≤ ℓ ≤ 30) temperature anisotropy measurements [12], which we refer to as low-ℓ CMB TT.
For CMB lensing, we combine the Planck PR4 [38] and
ACT DR6 [39] data, following [39]. Moreover, we supplement these cosmological datasets with robust constraints
on the reionization history from quasar damping wings
(QSO DW; [40-43]) and the two lowest-redshift Lyman-α
and Lyman-β dark pixel measurements from [44]3) .
We employ a physically motivated Gompertzian reionization model [27] to reconstruct the global neutral hydrogen fraction xH i (z) as a function of redshift. In contrast to the standard tanh prescription, which models
reionization as a symmetric sigmoid with an often fixed
duration and thus a single free parameter corresponding
to the midpoint of reionization, the Gompertzian model
describes reionization with an asymmetric sigmoid characterized by two free parameters. Its main advantages
compared to the hyperbolic tangent prescriptions are
that the model is physically motivated as it agrees well
with the expectations from EoR simulations and it is
well-suited for joint analyses of CMB and EoR observations. The self-consistent inclusion of robust EoR measurements improves constraints on τreio , thereby reducing
degeneracies with cosmological parameters. We recap
the Gompertzian reionization model in Appendix A.
The Gompertzian reionization model has been shown
to accurately reproduce the reionization history of simulations spanning a range of astrophysical prescriptions
[27]. These simulations, however, assume ΛCDM. This
approximation is well justified because cosmic reionization occurs deep within the matter-dominated era, where
the impact of dark energy should be negligible. Although the exact timing of the transition from matter
domination to dark energy domination depends weakly
on the underlying reionization model, the effect on the

Figure 2 shows the posterior distributions of τreio for
the different models. Overall, we find a preference for
lower values of τreio than those reported by [5], reflecting the use of a physically motivated and observationally
supported reionization model. In contrast to the nearly
symmetric narrowing of the posterior from ΛT to w0 wa T,
the transition from ΛG to w0 wa G exhibits a noticeably
asymmetric narrowing driven by the inclusion of EoR
observations. As discussed in Section 4, this behavior
ultimately arises from the interplay between constraints
provided by CMB lensing and EoR observations.
In the absence of large-scale CMB polarization data,
CMB lensing helps break the As e−2τreio degeneracy between the amplitude of the primordial power spectrum
As and τreio present in the high-ℓ CMB temperature and
polarization power spectra. CMB lensing constrains
the combination σ8 Ω0.25
m , where σ8 is the present-day
root-mean-square amplitude of the matter density field
smoothed over scales of 8 h−1 Mpc6) . Consequently, it
also tightly constrains the combination of As and Ωm .
Interestingly, our model that does not include EoR
observations has the same central value as the w0 wa T
model. The ΛG* posterior shape is symmetric given
that it does not have the additional constraints at the
low-end from, particularly, dark pixel upper limits. The
inclusion of EoR measurements breaks the symmetry in
the posterior and shifts τreio to smaller values than those
obtained from ΛT and ΛG*. Note that the broad symmetric posteriors of ΛT and ΛG* are in good agreement,
the usage of a physically motivated model does shifts the
posterior to slightly smaller values while also reducing
the spread slightly. In terms of cosmological parameters, the shift is due to ΛT yielding larger As than the
two other ΛCDM models, which requires larger τreio to
comply with high-ℓ CMB data.

5 Conclusions
We present a joint analysis of CMB, BAO, and reionization observations excluding large-scale CMB polarization data. Our analysis adopts a Gompertzian reionization model [27], a physically motivated framework calibrated against reionization simulations and designed to
jointly fit CMB and reionization observations [18].
We find that including quasar damping wing and dark
pixel constraints on xH i yields larger values of τreio than
those reported in [8, 9], which reconstructed xH i (z) directly from a heterogeneous compilation of EoR observations. Nevertheless, in our analysis the optical
depth decreases when extending ΛCDM to w0 wa CDM,
with ∆τreio = −0.010. Interestingly, this trend is also
present in other works. For instance, Ref. [5] results
yield ∆τreio = −0.003, while Ref. [8] likewise found a decrease in τreio when moving from ΛCDM to w0 wa CDM,
although the shift was small (∆τreio = −0.0006) for their
combination of BAO, BBN, and a broad compilation of
xH i measurements.
Our ΛCDM analysis excludes high values of the optical depth to reionization (τreio ≈ 0.09) at more than
1σ. Moreover, we found that the dynamical dark energy
model provides a better fit to the data.
Notably, our w0 wa CDM analysis yields a value of τreio
that is consistent with analyses including large-scale
CMB polarization data (see Figure 2), while simultaneously providing an excellent match to multiple independent measurements of the reionization history that
were not included in the likelihood computation (see
Figure 3). Despite excluding large-scale CMB polarization data, incorporating quasar damping wing and dark
pixel xH i constraints together with a physically motivated reionization model still leaves a ⪆ 2σ preference
for w0 wa CDM over ΛCDM (see Figure 5). Finally, although the increase in τreio is insuﬀicient to substantially
alleviate the dark energy tension, it is large enough to
resolve the apparent preference for a negative sum of
neutrino masses.
We conclude that, even if an undiscovered systematic
were present in large-scale CMB polarization spectra, it
would not by itself restore consistency with ΛCDM. Reconciling ΛCDM with the data would also require multiple independent EoR observations to be affected by
previously unidentified systematics in a mutually consistent manner. Nevertheless, relaxing some of the physical assumptions underlying ΛCDM could help alleviate
the remaining tension within our framework. For instance, departing from the cold dark matter paradigm
introduces additional freedom to modify the energy in-
