                                                                                            ABSTRACT
                                          We present accurate Monte Carlo computations of the X-ray secondary electron heating, ionization, and
                                        excitation of H and He gas in interstellar space and in quasar emission-line clouds. The fraction of energy
                                        deposited in each form is sensitive to the background ionization fraction, x = n(H+)/n(Htot), and can affect the
                                        temperature, ionization state, and line emissivities at large depths in X-ray photoionized clouds. We provide
                                        analytic fits for these energy fractions over the range 0.0001 < x < 1 for primary electron energies up to many
                                        keV. In both broad-line and narrow-line clouds, emission lines sensitive to the energy budget and electron
                                        density may be strongly affected.
                                        Subject headings: galaxies: nuclei — line formation — quasars

                                                                                                      APPENDIX
                                                                                         ELECTRON ENERGY LOSSES
                             The energy degradation of a primary electron in a partially ionized gas is generally understood as a competition between
                          Coulomb collisions with thermal electrons and collision excitation/ionization of neutrals (H i, He i). As the ionization fraction rises,
                          more of the primary’s energy goes into heat and less into excitations and ionizations.
                             However, one aspect of collisional ionization is important for understanding energy degradation of high-energy (E $> 100 eV)
                          electrons. When an energetic electron excites H i, He i, or He n, it loses a small amount of energy, typically 10.2, 20, or 40.8 eV.
                          However, in collisional ionization, the electron typically loses a large amount, owing to the high-energy tail in the energy
                          distribution of secondary electrons (Opal, Peterson, and Beatty 1971; Shull 1979). The average energy of these secondaries is much
                          larger than the values of 8 to 14 eV assumed for much lower energy primaries (Spitzer and Scott 1969; Habing and Goldsmith 1971).
                             We now discuss the energy dependence of the loss processes. At high energies, the cross sections for Coulomb collisions with
                          electrons (cree) and for excitation of H i (aeH) take the asymptotic (or Born approximation) forms,
