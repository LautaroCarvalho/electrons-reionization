================================================================================
HOW THE IONIZATION EVENTS ARE COUNTED
Direct collisional ionization and the photoionization cascades of secondary
inverse-Compton photons
================================================================================
Written 2026-09-02.  Describes the bookkeeping implemented in
cascade_traj.py / photon_cascade.py (and re-derived independently in
Notebooks/redo/stage2_cascade.py).  Numbers quoted here are from the
independent re-run; see Notebooks/redo/RESULTS.txt for the full output.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0.  THE COMMON PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
An "ionization event" is one act of removing the electron from a hydrogen atom.
Both branches count events by integrating an event RATE along the primary's
cooling trajectory, and both close the count with a recursion, because every
event creates a new energetic electron that ionizes in its own right.  The
general shape is always

    dY/dt = (rate of events) x [ 1 + <Y(energy given to the freed electron)> ]
                                 ^         ^
                                 |         everything that electron will ever do
                                 this event itself

The two branches differ only in what produces the event and in what energy the
freed electron starts with.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  DIRECT COLLISIONAL BRANCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1  The rate
-------------
At time t the primary has kinetic energy K_e(t), given by the seven-channel
ODE, so ionizing collisions occur at

    R_ion = n_HI(z) v(K_e) sigma_ion(K_e)

with sigma_ion the relativistic binary-encounter-Bethe (RBEB) cross-section.
Integrating R_ion alone gives N_1, the primary's own ionizations: the dotted
curves of Fig. 4a BEFORE the shower is added.

1.2  The recursion
------------------
Each collision also ejects a secondary of energy eps distributed as p(eps|K),
the normalised BEB singly differential cross-section.  That secondary is an
ordinary cosmic-ray electron, so it contributes Y(eps,z) further ionizations:

    dY_e/dt = n_HI v sigma_ion(K_e) [ 1 + INT deps p(eps|K_e) Y(eps,z) ]

1.3  Why this closes without iteration
--------------------------------------
By the BEB convention the "secondary" is the SLOWER of the two outgoing
electrons, so

    eps <= (K - B_H)/2 < K        strictly.

Y at energy K therefore only ever calls Y at strictly lower energies.  Marching
upward in K makes the fixed point exact in a single sweep: no iteration, no
truncation of generations.  The tree terminates because sigma_ion = 0 below
13.6 eV, so a low-energy branch simply ends.

1.4  Why there is no double counting
------------------------------------
This is the part that has to be right.  The parent's ionization loss rate is

    Lambda_ion = sigma_ion (B_H + <eps>)

i.e. the parent is charged the binding energy AND the kinetic energy it hands
to the secondary.  The secondary then spends exactly that eps on its own
trajectory.  So each joule is removed from one particle and deposited by one
particle, and each event is counted once: the parent gets credit for its
child's ionizations only through <Y>, never by keeping the child's energy as
well.

Verified by closure of the energy budget over the whole cascade,

    13.6 Y + H + X + E_esc = K_ini

to 0.4 % along the trajectory, and to 0.03 % in the static CSDA version, where
there is no ODE tolerance.  (H = Coulomb heat, X = excitation, E_esc =
IC + synchrotron + bremsstrahlung + adiabatic, all summed over generations.)

1.5  Two details that are easy to miss
--------------------------------------
(a) <Y(eps,z)> is evaluated at the redshift WHERE THE COLLISION HAPPENED, not
    at z_i.  This is irrelevant for eps <~ 1e5 eV, since those secondaries
    thermalise in Delta z ~ 0.002, but it matters for the energetic secondaries
    of a fast primary.

(b) The parent's energy loss uses the MEAN <eps>, while the cascade uses the
    full distribution p(eps|K).  That is a mean-field treatment of the parent
    and a distributional treatment of the children.  It conserves energy
    exactly and is standard practice, but it suppresses event-to-event
    fluctuations: Y is a mean yield, not a sampled one.

1.6  Size of the effect
-----------------------
The shower multiplies the primary's own count by

    1.00 at   40 eV
    1.25 at    1 keV
    1.80 at  1e5 eV
    2.33 (z_i = 20) and 2.52 (z_i = 10) at the top of the grid

i.e. the "factor 1.02-2.5, rising with K_ini" quoted in the manuscript.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  INVERSE-COMPTON PHOTON BRANCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1  Why a second counter is needed at all
------------------------------------------
Above ~1e5 eV the electron stops depositing energy in the gas and starts
radiating it: f_IC > 0.99 above 100 MeV.  The energy-loss curve tells you HOW
MUCH is radiated but not HOW MANY of those photons can ionize, which depends
entirely on where they land in the spectrum.  Hence the differential
Klein-Nishina spectrum dN_gamma/(dt dE_gamma) of Blumenthal & Gould (1970),
whose energy integral reproduces the L_IC used in the ODE to 0.14 %.  (Of that
residual, 0.115 % is the rounded constant U_CMB_0 = 4.17e-14 J/m^3 versus the
exact a T^4 = 4.1748e-14; the remainder is the gamma^2/(gamma^2-1) = 1/beta^2
offset between the emitted power and the net loss rate, which is exact and was
verified symbolically.)

2.2  The window
---------------
A photon counts only if  B_H <= E_gamma <= E_max :

  * the LOWER edge is kinematic.  Below 13.6 eV the photon cannot ionize
    hydrogen at all, and its energy joins the sub-ionizing background.  This is
    the "dead band" K_2 < K_e < K_3 of the long draft.

  * the UPPER edge is an opacity statement.  Above ~1 keV the photoionization
    mean free path exceeds c/H, so the photon free-streams and its energy
    leaves the medium.  This is K_4.

Two ceilings are tabulated: the fixed E_max = 1 keV (the headline assumption)
and the redshift-dependent interaction horizon, E_gamma,hor(z) = 1820 eV at
z = 20 down to 983 eV at z = 5.5.

2.3  The counting rule
----------------------
Inside the window each photon is credited with EXACTLY ONE photoionization.
Note what this means: sigma_pi does NOT enter the count.  It enters only in
locating E_max.  Inside the band the absorption probability is set to 1 by
fiat.

That assumption was tested photon-weighted (i.e. weighting by the actual IC
spectrum, not uniformly across the band).  The probability that a band photon
is absorbed AS A PHOTOIONIZATION within one Hubble length is

    fixed 1 keV band  :  0.99         at both z_i    -> rule good to ~1 %
    horizon ceiling   :  0.87 - 0.95                 -> rule optimistic by
                                                        5-13 %

because near the horizon edge up to a third of the interactions are Compton
scatters rather than photoionizations.  Consequence: the quoted +13 % / +37 %
increase from adopting the horizon ceiling is itself an overestimate.

2.4  The recursion
------------------
The photoionization leaves a photoelectron with K_e = E_gamma - B_H, which is
an ordinary electron and runs the same cascade as Sect. 1:

    dY_gamma/dt = INT_{B_H}^{E_max} dE_gamma (dN_gamma/dE_gamma)
                                     x [ 1 + Y_e(E_gamma - B_H, z) ]

2.5  Why the recursion is only one level deep on the photon side
----------------------------------------------------------------
A photoelectron has K_e < 1 keV, three to four decades below K_3 = 4-8 MeV,
the energy at which an electron can up-scatter a CMB photon above 13.6 eV.  So
photoelectrons never produce ionizing IC photons of their own: the photon
branch feeds INTO the electron branch and stops there.  There is no
photon-photon recursion to solve.

2.6  The secondaries emit too
-----------------------------
Collisional secondaries are themselves electrons that inverse-Compton scatter,
so the photon accumulators carry the same shower term as the electron ones:

    dY_gamma/dt  +=  n_HI v sigma_ion <Y_gamma(eps,z)>

CODE NOTE: in photon_cascade.py the Ngamma accumulator omits this term while
Yph includes it, so the published "3.6 ionizations per productive photon" mixes
conventions in numerator and denominator.  Numerically it changes nothing (with
both accumulators consistent the ratio is still 3.60), but the two are not on
the same footing as published.

2.7  Anatomy of the "3.6 ionizations per productive photon"
------------------------------------------------------------
That number is a trajectory average over wildly different regimes, and it is
worth seeing why it is small.  For a single electron of energy K_e at z = 20:

  K_e [eV]   peak IC energy   fraction of ALL     <E_gamma>    ionizations
             4 g^2 <eps>      photons in band     in band      per photon
  --------   --------------   ---------------     ---------    -----------
  1e7             22 eV            0.17              22 eV        1.04
  3e7            190 eV            0.76              82 eV        2.59
  1e8            2.1 keV           0.73             361 eV       10.60
  1e9            204 keV           0.024            495 eV       14.46
  1e10            20 MeV           0.0003           506 eV       14.79

The photon COUNT is dominated by the phase in which the cooling electron sits
near 2-5 x 1e7 eV, where the band captures most of the emitted photons but each
one is only tens of eV and its photoelectron barely ionizes.  The
high-multiplicity photons exist but are rare.  Averaged along the trajectory:

    z_i = 20 :  7.90e5 productive photons, 2.84e6 ionizations -> 3.597
    z_i = 10 :  1.09e6 productive photons, 3.92e6 ionizations -> 3.604


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  WHY THE TWO COUNTS ARE ADDITIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
They are disjoint physical events: a bound electron removed by an electron
impact, versus one removed by a photon.  The energy that feeds them is booked
once each.  Lambda_ion removes the collisional part from the electron, L_IC
removes the radiated part, and only the fraction of the radiated part that
lands inside the window is allowed to come back as ionizations.  Y = Y_e +
Y_gamma is therefore a sum of independent counts, not a re-partition of one.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.  WHAT THE COUNTING ASSUMES  (worth stating explicitly in the paper)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. One absorption per band photon, instantaneous and local.  Good to ~1 % for
   the fixed band, 5-13 % for the horizon ceiling.  It also ignores photon
   redshifting between emission and absorption, which is conservative:
   redshifting a photon RAISES sigma_pi and so makes absorption more likely.

2. Hard band edges.  Real radiative transfer would smear both, and Compton
   down-scattering of photons above E_max would return some energy that is
   currently discarded entirely.

3. Y counts ionization EVENTS, not net ionized fraction: no recombination is
   tracked anywhere.

4. x_e is frozen at 1e-4.  Each ionization raises it, and the cascade is
   sensitive to it: at x_e = 1e-2 the same cascade gives W = 43 eV instead of
   35.3 eV, and the heat-per-ionization rises from 6.5 to 15.8 eV at 1 keV.
   The counter has no feedback loop.

5. Pure hydrogen.  Helium would add ionization channels, extra opacity for the
   band photons, and its own photoelectrons.

6. The yield is a mean over the secondary distribution, not a Monte-Carlo
   sample, so it carries no variance information.
